μ Cassiopeiae is a nearby (~25 light-year) metal-poor G-type dwarf with a large
space velocity, marking it as a member of an older, kinematically hot stellar
population. Its metallicity ([Fe/H] ≈ −0.7) places it well below the Sun.

## Why it's here

μ Cas is an astrometric binary: the primary is orbited by a low-mass M-dwarf
companion, and the orbit yields a dynamical mass for the primary. A nearby,
metal-poor star with a directly measured mass is exactly the kind of anchor
needed to test the mass–luminosity relation and interior/mixing-length
prescriptions at subsolar metallicity.
