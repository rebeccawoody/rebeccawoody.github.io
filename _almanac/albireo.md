Albireo (β Cygni) marks the head of the Swan in the constellation Cygnus, at
the foot of the asterism known as the Northern Cross. To the naked eye it looks
like a single star of about third magnitude, but even a modest telescope
resolves it into a striking pair separated by roughly 35 arcseconds — often
called the finest color-contrast double in the sky.

## The pair

- **Albireo A** — the brighter, golden-amber component (apparent magnitude
  ~3.1), a K-type bright giant. It is itself a close binary: a cool giant
  paired with a hotter B-type star that are not separable in most amateur
  instruments.
- **Albireo B** — the fainter, blue-white component (apparent magnitude ~5.1),
  a hot, fast-rotating B-type main-sequence star.

The vivid gold-versus-blue contrast comes from the large difference in surface
temperature between the cool giant and the hot main-sequence star — roughly
4,400 K for the amber primary against some 13,000 K for the blue companion. The
colors are a direct, naked-telescope illustration of the relationship between a
star's temperature and its color.

## A true binary, or a chance alignment?

For a long time Albireo A and B were assumed to form a wide, gravitationally
bound system with an orbital period of many thousands of years. Astrometry from
the *Gaia* mission complicated that picture: the measured parallaxes and proper
motions of A and B differ enough to suggest they may lie at meaningfully
different distances and move independently — i.e. an *optical double*, a
line-of-sight coincidence rather than a physical pair. The interpretation is
not fully settled — Albireo A's own inner binarity makes its astrometry harder
to pin down — and it is a nice reminder that even famous, familiar objects can
hold open problems.

## Finding it

Albireo sits at the southern end of the Northern Cross, almost on a line between
Vega and Altair, and is easy to locate on a summer or autumn evening in the
northern sky. Almost any small telescope — even steadily held binoculars at
higher magnification — will split the pair and show the color contrast.
